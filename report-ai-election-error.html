Did an AI give you wrong election information?

Chatbots and AI search tools now answer voters' questions every day. Some of those answers are wrong: not because anyone set out to mislead, but because of how these systems work. Tell us what happened. Every report becomes part of a public record.

  • It gave you last cycle's deadline, or rules from a law that has since changed.
  • It pointed you to a forum post, a fake news site or a source that turned out to be bot-made.
  • It invented a polling place, a candidate position, a quote or a law.

    Why this exists

    Most worry about AI and elections is about bad actors: deepfakes, fake robocalls, influence campaigns. This project looks at something quieter. Ordinary AI tools, used the ordinary way, sometimes give voters wrong answers.

    A model can repeat rules from its training data that have since changed. It can pull in a forum post or a site built by a bot network and present it as fact. It can invent a detail outright. None of that needs a bad actor. It just needs a voter asking a normal question.

    Test labs can show what a model might do. They cannot show what it did, to real people, in the weeks before a real election. Only reports from real use can do that. That is what we collect.

    What happens to your report

    Reports are stored privately. We review them, remove personal details, and look for patterns across AI products, states and types of error. We will publish findings and anonymized examples for the public, reporters, researchers and the people who write the rules for these tools.

    What we keep

    What you type and upload, plus the rough area your connection came from (country, state and city). We do not store your internet address. We never sell or share your contact details.

    This is not an official election resource

    For voting rules, check your state or local elections office. You can find yours at vote.gov.
